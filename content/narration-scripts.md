# Silent Cue — Narration Scripts

These are master scripts for audio/video narration. All narration is delivered in the first person by the anonymous narrator. Tone: low, unhurried, measured. Cinematic pauses indicated with [pause].

---

## Script 01 — Brand Introduction / Origin

*For: YouTube opening video, homepage ambient audio, email welcome dispatch.*

---

I'm not going to tell you who I am.

[pause]

What I am going to tell you is where I've been, and what I learned there.

For a long time, I spent my evenings in rooms most people will never see. Not because they couldn't find them. Because they weren't looking in the right direction. Rooms with bad light and good table felt. Rooms where the conversation was slow and the money moved quietly. Rooms where a man's hands told you everything you needed to know about his relationship with pressure before he ever got down on a shot.

[pause]

I played pool for years. And then I stopped playing pool and started studying it. The distinction matters.

Playing is about the outcome. Studying is about what the game reveals. And what it reveals, if you watch long enough and stay quiet enough, is everything.

Not about pool. About people. About the specific kind of honesty that only emerges when the stakes are real and there's nowhere to hide.

[pause]

I built Silent Cue to pass on what I learned. Not to everyone. To those who are already asking the right kind of questions.

If you found this, you probably know what I mean.

[pause]

The game was never about the table.

The table only reveals the player.

## Script 02 — Module 01 Intro: The Composed Mind

*For: Course Module 01 opening audio.*

---

The first time I watched a truly composed player, I didn't understand what I was seeing.

[pause]

I'd seen technically skilled players before. I'd seen players who made difficult shots look routine. But this was different. This man wasn't making it look easy. He was making it look inevitable.

The pace of everything around him was normal — the sounds of the room, the movement of other people, the ordinary noise of a pool hall at ten o'clock. But inside his radius, there was something else. A kind of organized quiet that had nothing to do with the volume of the room.

[pause]

I spent years learning what that was. And the answer surprised me, because it had almost nothing to do with pool.

The composed mind is not a talent. It is an architecture. It is a set of systems, built deliberately, that allow a person to access their full capability when the ambient conditions are working against them.

Pressure is not the enemy of performance. [pause] Poor preparation for pressure is the enemy of performance.

In this module, we're going to build your architecture. Not the architecture of the shot — you already have technical resources for that. The architecture of the mind that delivers the shot when the room is watching and the money is on the line.

[pause]

Let's begin.

## Script 03 — The Pressure Stack (Framework Introduction)

*For: YouTube essay, Module 04 lesson.*

---

Pressure doesn't arrive all at once.

[pause]

That's the mistake most players make in their understanding of it. They think pressure is a thing that descends at a specific moment — the last ball, the final rack, the shot that decides it. And so they prepare for that moment, or they don't. Either way, they're always surprised by how heavy it is when it arrives.

But pressure is a stack. It accumulates.

[pause]

It starts before you walk in the room. The awareness of who your opponent is. What you've heard about them. What happened last time. The drive over. The way you ate that morning. All of it goes into the foundation.

Then: the stakes. What this match means. Money, reputation, ranking, pride. Each one adds weight. Not always consciously. Usually not consciously. The body keeps score when the mind stops paying attention.

Then the crowd, if there is one. Observation changes performance. That's not opinion — that's neurochemistry. Being watched activates a self-monitoring process that competes for the same cognitive resources your technique requires.

Then your recent shots. The miss three racks ago that you told yourself you forgot. You didn't forget. It's in the stack.

[pause]

By the time you're standing over the last ball, you're not carrying the weight of one shot. You're carrying everything you've allowed to accumulate since you woke up that morning.

The skilled pressure player doesn't have lighter stacks. [pause] They have better systems for managing what's in them.

That is what this module is about.

## Script 06 — Atmosphere / Music Direction

*Notes for any audio producer or composer working on Silent Cue.*

### Overall Direction
- Genre: Jazz-influenced ambient / atmospheric trap
- Reference points: Bohren & Der Club of Gore, Lorn, Flying Lotus (downtempo), Nicolas Jaar
- Tempo: 60–75 BPM for background content. 80–90 BPM for video essays.
- Key signature: Minor preferred. Diminished and chromatic movement welcome.

### Instruments
- **Double bass**: Bowed, subtle. The anchor frequency of the brand.
- **Piano**: Sparse. Single notes or minor chords with room to breathe.
- **Brushed drums**: Very low in the mix. Texture, not rhythm.
- **Vinyl crackle**: Always present. Thin layer. Connects to underground/archive aesthetic.
- **Cello**: For emotional tension. Cinematic moments.
- **Sub-bass frequencies**: Felt more than heard. Presence without aggression.

### What to Avoid
- Drop builds
- EDM structure
- Bright synth pads
- Any kind of "epic" orchestral swell
- Hard 4/4 kick patterns
- Happy/major key resolution

### Specific Cues
- **Intro atmosphere** (before narration begins): 8–16 seconds of pure texture. No melody.
- **Narration underscore**: Music retreats significantly — listener should not be aware of it, only of its absence when it leaves.
- **Emotional peaks**: Music lifts slightly in presence. Never volume. Presence.
- **Outro**: Music fades before the final line of narration. The last sentence lands in silence.
